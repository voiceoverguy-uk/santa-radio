---
name: Nice List certificate artwork scope
description: Owner-approved design and privacy boundaries for personalised certificates.
---
Certificates and personalised letters are gifts reserved for the paid Studio offer, not part of the basic free greeting. Public visual examples may appear inside the clearly labelled paid Studio promotion on free-message pages; never put certificate/letter delivery controls or promises into the free greeting results or cooldown.

The promotional certificate and letter should change to the same example name every 10 seconds, with a writing effect on both. Remove “Next example” and “Show full letter now”; retain enlargement, showing the complete document for reading.

**Why:** The owner explicitly requested a ten-second name cycle shared by the certificate and letter.

**How to apply:** Keep both documents synchronised, finish the writing within each cycle, and preserve reading/enlargement pauses, reduced-motion support and matching to deliberately played example audio. This remains a visual preview, not generated delivery.

**Why:** The owner explicitly superseded the earlier free-certificate requirements while retaining the approved artwork and reusable generator for the paid product, then requested a matched animated certificate and letter preview within its promotion.

**How to apply:** Preserve the certificate assets and generation utilities. The owner authorised a visual trial of the certificate and letter, not paid delivery, letter PDFs, checkout changes or new entitlements. Keep coming-soon and incomplete-bundle wording truthful.

Preserve the supplied certificate's foreground artwork except for the explicitly authorised removal of the decorative top Santa Radio logo and modest enlargement/repositioning of “Official Nice List Certificate”. Keep all other Santa Radio references. Its background is authorised to use the actual homepage snowy North Pole image across the full A4 landscape page, not the original blank emerald left extension. Personalise with the completed recording's canonical display name and the approved humorous Nice status line; no year or other extra wording.

**Why:** The owner explicitly approved the larger decorative Santa Claus signature, gold wax-style seal, gold line and absence of a name-field rectangle, then authorised replacing the background while retaining those elements. Their later heading feedback explicitly superseded preservation of the top wordmark only.

**How to apply:** Keep personalisation local to the browser, separate from audio allowance and public invitation sharing. Certificate failure must not affect audio; do not add recipient storage or remote certificate delivery.

Include “Based on behaviour this year, you are awarded XX% Nice status!” beneath the main heading, with a randomly chosen whole-number percentage from 75 to 95 inclusive for each newly generated certificate.

**Why:** The owner requested this as a funny addition to the certificate.

**How to apply:** Keep it part of the actual personalised PDF and website examples, not just interface text. Generate a new random draw for each certificate download, including repeated downloads, without regenerating audio or consuming message allowance.

Named certificate PDFs supplied for previews are examples, not replacement personalisation masters. An audio attachment alongside those examples does not authorise replacing the existing example audio.

**Why:** The owner requested named examples as promotional previews while explicitly keeping both the personalised PDF generator and example recordings unchanged.

**How to apply:** Edward and Ayla should match the current master and real local personalisation process, not the old supplied example artwork. Keep original uploads intact and treat example audio as separate.

Keep the promotional certificate box aligned left with the introduction and sized to fit its text area, not centred at a strict percentage reduction. Keep automatic rotation, no name-selection buttons, no pause control and no “Showing…” status line. Clicking the visible example enlarges that example.

**Why:** After requesting a 30% reduction, the owner clarified that it looked too small and should align left and fit the outlined text area. They explicitly removed the extra controls and status text.

**How to apply:** Preserve enlargement and reduced-motion behaviour when refining the compact preview.

When a visitor plays a sample greeting, show the certificate for that sample’s name and hold it for the duration of playback. Release it back to the normal rotation when playback finishes or is stopped.

**Why:** The owner asked for the certificate to jump to the name about to play instead of continuing to show unrelated names during the recording.

**How to apply:** Keep the pairing consistent on the homepage and dedicated message page. Matching the sample is an explicit visitor action, so it still applies with reduced motion; only automatic rotation remains suppressed.

The background fade trial means retaining 80% of the existing treated snowy scene and blending 20% towards its existing deep dark teal, not towards white or by reducing the whole certificate's opacity.

**Why:** The requested trial is intended to make the existing light gold wording and name stand out while keeping the foreground artwork untouched.

**How to apply:** Apply the same background-only treatment to the master and website examples. Keep before/after comparisons available without adding a visitor-facing setting.

Embed the supplied font in full rather than subsetting it.

**Why:** The fontkit subset writer omitted visible letters from this particular supplied static font even though PDF text extraction returned the correct name. Full embedding rendered correctly.

**How to apply:** Verify the actual rasterised output, not only ToUnicode extraction. Keep full embedding unless a replacement implementation is visually checked with normal, long and accented/punctuated names.

Font changes must preserve both the visible name and its extracted PDF text.

**Why:** The supplied script font could render contextual ligatures correctly while PDF extraction substituted unrelated Unicode letters. A visual-only check would miss incorrect spelling for copying and assistive readers.

**How to apply:** When changing fonts or shaping options, compare extracted canonical names as well as actual ink bounds and rasterised PDFs, including long names containing common letter pairs.
