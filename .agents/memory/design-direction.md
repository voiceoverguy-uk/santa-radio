Do not show a breadcrumb/path trail on Christmas artist/song pages.

**Why:** The user said “remove the path. we don't need to show it”.

**How to apply:** Keep those pages free of visible breadcrumb trails; retain ordinary navigation and the back-to-music link.

Show the current artist's artwork on the right of the homepage hero during desktop radio playback, with a thin frame and the same gold hover glow as other homepage boxes. Also show it beneath the countdown on mobile while playing; hide it when paused.

**Why:** The user originally requested desktop-only artwork, then requested mobile artwork too after showing the unused space beneath the countdown.

**How to apply:** Preserve the North Pole backdrop and existing player; use shared artist artwork and Santa fallback without adding duplicate song-detail blocks.

The user finds the initial Santa Radio rebuild "a bit boring" and wants the colours and appearance of https://santa-radio.base44.app/.

The user approved the redesign plan and specifically asked to make buttons and fonts the same as the Base44 version.

Use the user's latest supplied Santa Radio logo consistently throughout the site, including the header, footer and page wordmarks, not font-based approximations.

**Why:** The user repeated that the Santa Radio logo must stay the same throughout and supplied the artwork to use.

**How to apply:** Treat the reference's premium winter setting and rich colours as the visual target when redesigning this project. The uploaded prompt also describes a larger membership product; do not assume a request to review its design authorises implementing all of its commercial features.

Santa's rotating notes should use playful, family-friendly British humour about elves, reindeer, biscuits and everyday North Pole mishaps.

**Why:** The user explicitly praised the generated lines as funny and requested more in the same style.

**How to apply:** Keep future additions in that voice, starting with “Santa here... ” rather than generic promotional copy.

Keep Santa's notes as plain text in the Contact Santa Radio section, not in the hero or a speech bubble. Do not include “Sent with Elfie”. Do not add a separate song-details section; the existing now-playing artwork card may include a compact live caption.

**Why for the artwork caption:** The owner explicitly requested the artist on the first line and song title on the second beneath the playing artwork, superseding the earlier preference against repeating track details there.

**How to apply the caption:** Display actual live metadata independently of catalogue matching, retaining the square picture and its overlaid information button. Keep the existing desktop/mobile placement and playing-only visibility.

**Why:** After trying the compact bubble, the user asked to revert to “the last one as just text” and place it in the Contact Santa Radio section.

**How to apply:** Keep the space content-sized, without a large reserved area. Show three animated dots briefly, then fade the complete line in, with no letter-by-letter typing. Leave a clear gap below Send Email; preserve rotating humorous messages and reduced-motion support.

**Why for motion and sizing:** The user asked for a fade instead of typing, then explicitly asked to restore the animated three dots and move the message further down from Send Email.

On mobile, the radio player should start minimised on a fresh page load, even if the tab previously remembered an expanded player. Keep manual expansion available and do not collapse it on every internal navigation.

**Why:** The user asked for a minimised mobile starting state. Applying this at initial load avoids a saved expanded setting obscuring the phone page, without interrupting browsing.

**How to apply:** Preserve the mobile initial-load rule separately from the desktop remembered preference when changing player controls or persistence.

The compact player's information area and empty space should expand its details. Play/Pause should control audio only, without expanding the player.

**Why:** The user asked for a larger expansion target than the small arrow, explicitly excluding the Listen Live/Pause button.

**How to apply:** Keep separate accessible controls for opening details and playing audio; changing player size must not interrupt playback.

Keep the hero countdown compact and visually secondary to the heading and buttons, directly on the artwork with just timer values and labels, without an outer card or introductory headings and copy.

**Why:** The user approved the swapped countdown/Santa-texting arrangement, but found the countdown cluttered and progressively removed its outer box, eyebrow, heading and supporting sentence. They repeatedly asked to make the countdown smaller, specifically on mobile even after the desktop reduction.

**How to apply:** Preserve that simplicity in future hero changes; do not treat the richer North Pole direction as a request to box every element. Keep the mobile timer noticeably narrower than the content area, rather than stretching its cells to fill it.

Keep the hero's station/all-year tagline on one line on mobile.

**Why:** The user explicitly objected to “round” wrapping onto a lower line and asked for one line.

**How to apply:** Give the tagline the available width and scale it gently on the narrowest phones rather than clipping or shortening its wording.

Prioritise reaching the tracker and soundboard quickly over additional introductory sections on the homepage.

**Why:** The user wanted to remove repeated introductory copy “to get to the fun stuff quicker like the tracker and the sound board”.

**How to apply:** Avoid reintroducing a large promotional section between the introduction and interactive content unless the user requests it.

Snow should be on by default, with the On/Off switch in the footer rather than the top navigation. Continue respecting a visitor's reduced-motion setting by default, but an explicit Snow On choice should enable snowfall only, without enabling other decorative motion.

**Why for placement:** The user asked to move the switch to the footer to clean up the top navigation bar.

**Why:** The user reported that snow worked on their Safari Mac but not for other visitors. Reduced-motion settings could hide snow permanently despite the switch saying On. Accessibility defaults must not make this deliberate opt-in control ineffective.

**How to apply:** Keep the switch label aligned with actual snowfall. Treat historical automatically saved On settings as defaults, not proof that a visitor explicitly opted into motion.

The site's shared gold box glow includes the Santa soundboard buttons, not just passive cards and panels.

**Why:** The user clarified that the soundboard boxes should glow too after the broader all-boxes change omitted them.

**How to apply:** Do not exclude the soundboard simply because its boxes are buttons. Preserve audio controls and distinguish playing/disabled states when adjusting decorative effects.

The user loves the current snowy North Pole village and northern-lights background.

**Why:** They explicitly said “I love the background you made” when requesting a distant Santa flyby.

**How to apply:** Preserve that backdrop when tuning the flyby; keep the silhouette as a separate decorative overlay rather than replacing or baking it into the artwork.

Mugshot profile photographs should share the Chuckle Brothers' display proportions, including taller originals, without cropping or stretching.

**Why:** The user noticed Christine Lampard was taller and approved standardising the seven exceptions to the other 642 profiles after being offered full-photo preservation.

**How to apply:** Keep a consistent profile frame when adding photographs with different proportions; do not silently crop originals to make them match.

Link-preview graphics should use the newer straight gold Santa Radio logo, not the curved logo, and the site's green colours. The supplied Santa-with-headphones illustration can be included.

**Why:** The user said the graphic shown when pasting the site URL into an email looked weak and requested these brand elements.

**How to apply:** Use the supplied straight logo artwork for share-card designs, with clear branding that remains readable at email-thumbnail sizes.
