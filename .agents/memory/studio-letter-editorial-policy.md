---
name: Studio letter editorial policy
description: Owner's scope for contextual letters and catalogue confidentiality.
---

Use AI only as an editorial aid for a curated British-English library, not as a
live letter-writing service. Santa's reactions should fit the selected age,
activity, achievement, offering and destination, including adult believers and
deliberately silly gifts. “Extra celebration” means the existing achievement,
not an invented birthday or date.

**Why:** The owner requested context-sensitive reactions rather than the
universal age “already!” and food-style gratitude. They explicitly excluded
runtime AI, new recipient storage, recording changes and paid PDF fulfilment.

**How to apply:** Audit the current authorised non-name catalogue rather than
claiming a small public fixture is complete. Keep the full editorial inventory
server-side; unknown meanings need neutral prose and an editorial flag rather
than guessed expansions. Wording variation must survive spelling edits,
presentation changes and a trip back through the choice editor without using
or storing the recipient's name as its random seed.

Keep general festive story passages between selected details, not a letter in
which every line tries to be personalised. Finite curated variety must never
be described as guaranteed unique gifts.

**Why:** The owner wants the fuller, story-led feel restored without losing
contextual reactions; the short consecutive form-answer style was not enough.

**How to apply:** Preserve the one-page typography and contextual meaning.
UK writing must use the actual England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland
selections, with Ireland separate. New regional recordings in the existing
destination catalogue may be discovered before they have bespoke prose; use a
neutral named-place line and flag them privately rather than inventing facts.

Regional selections must not inherit prose for a country with the same name.
Use neutral named-place wording with the parent country unless a passage is
curated for that exact regional context.

**Why:** A regional label such as Georgia is ambiguous; country-only editorial
matching would attach the wrong place's story to an otherwise valid recording.

**How to apply:** Keep the audio selection's explicit parent relationship when
forming letter details. Do not infer the intended geography from a label alone.

The owner approved automatic, short local Santa-and-elf stories for selected
US states and UK-nation counties, with two or three curated alternatives.
Replace the neutral destination passage; add no questions, recordings or
recipient storage. Florida may mention Magic Kingdom and EPCOT; West Yorkshire
should have recognisable child-friendly local humour. Avoid pub/alcohol jokes
and assumptions that the recipient lives near or has visited a landmark.

**Why:** The owner wants recognisable local personalisation in the letter only,
with reviewed wording rather than runtime AI.

**How to apply:** Preserve Ireland/Northern Ireland distinctions and country-only,
fantasy and space writing. Inspect source and catalogue gaps privately rather
than claiming every geographical area is recorded. Budget regional prose with
the complete displayed region-and-parent label, not the passage alone: those
long labels can overflow a longest-combination A4 letter even when ordinary
examples fit. Shorten regional copy, not the approved typography or other scenes.

Include one short, silly Christmas joke per letter, chosen from 25 family-friendly jokes and introduced with “By the way, (NAME), do you like jokes?”

**Why:** The owner requested a line or two of Christmas humour in Santa's letter.

**How to apply:** Choose the joke with the letter's existing page-local variation, not from the child's name; keep it stable during edits and preserve the one-page letter layout.
